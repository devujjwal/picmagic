## 1. How to Create AI Photos With ChatGPT Using Your Own Photo

**Search intent:** Complete a first reference-photo-to-image workflow in ChatGPT. Secondary intents: choose a usable reference, copy/adapt a prompt and correct a poor result. **Likely next question:** “Which part do I change if the scene works but my face does not?”

**Reader verdict:** Yes, the revised page is a useful answer PicMagic can credibly provide. Its opening gives the workflow and identity limitation immediately. The reference section explains visible facial detail, even light, obstructions and when a wider body/clothing reference matters.

**Changes:** Replaced “reference-capable conversation” and “canonical prompt page” jargon with direct actions. Specified the attachment control and web drag/paste options, opening City Lights, copying and pasting its prompt, requesting an image, inspecting the result and correcting one mismatch. Separated generation from inspection. Clarified PicMagic's role and changed background-edit wording from a promised outcome to a requested constraint. Added the official image-input source.

**Original value / AEO / GEO:** City Lights is explained as a still subject against moving city activity, with advice to preserve that mechanism. The guide ties the actual copy action to reference-role instructions, contradictory age defaults and a concrete face-correction request. The quick answer, reference-selection paragraph and focused-edit example remain meaningful out of context.

**Visual and link assessment:** City Lights is an appropriate single illustrative result, with a readable face and visible street-motion contrast. It does not pretend to demonstrate a before/after identity test. The documented trial and reference-photo guide supply deeper context; double exposure remains owned by its existing guide. No prompt is duplicated in full.

**CTA:** **Try City Lights** → `/ai-prompts/men/city-lights-main-character-energy`. Retained: the article teaches this exact scene and the destination provides a usable prompt. It is one worked example, not a claim that this look suits everyone.

**Remaining weakness:** No current ChatGPT UI screenshot or new end-to-end likeness test. Official instructions and an explicit, qualified workflow are sufficient for publication; the page must not be presented as tested across every model/account. **READY TO PUBLISH.**

## 2. How to Keep Your Face the Same in AI-Generated Photos

**Search intent:** Diagnose identity drift and improve instructions. Secondary intents: retain age/complexion, adapt hair and expression, resolve conflicting styling and map multiple people. **Likely next question:** “What exact instruction should I replace or send next?”

**Reader verdict:** Yes. This is the strongest practical methodology article in the batch. It explains why an attractive scene can still be the wrong person, then gives a review order and corrective language without promising a face lock.

**Changes:** Made the quick answer include age and removing conflicts. Explained that `REFERENCE PRESENT` is PicMagic prompt text, not a special external-model command. Disclosed the actual approximately 22–30 fallback and the need to remove it when preserving actual age. Added the preview-jawline contradiction and K-drama example. Expanded source-person/output-position mapping to couples and groups. Added a specific nose/jaw correction and a reminder to recheck the whole image. Removed the contrived “tested ranking” FAQ; the evidence disclosure already covers that limitation.

**Original value / AEO / GEO:** The identity/style table covers facial structure, age cues, ethnicity, natural complexion, proportions and hairline versus pose, gaze, expression, clothes, environment and permitted hair arrangement. Particularly useful passages explain that cultural cinematography does not determine ethnicity; a source person's position differs from their requested output position; and appending a preservation instruction does not remove an earlier contradictory instruction. These are grounded in PicMagic's actual identity clauses, not invented test results.

**Visual and link assessment:** Change the Scene, Keep Yourself illustrates a restrained edit scope. Its one output is not likeness evidence. The reference-photo guide remains the general reference setup resource; this article owns troubleshooting and conflict diagnosis.

**CTA:** **Try an identity-aware look** → `/ai-prompts/men/change-the-scene-keep-yourself`. Retained: a limited background edit is a coherent next exercise after learning to separate identity from transformable attributes.

**Remaining weakness:** No measured improvement rates or legitimate before/after identity series. The article explicitly describes methodology. That limits authority claims, not the practical usefulness of its instructions. **READY TO PUBLISH.**
